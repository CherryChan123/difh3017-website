Reading Note: The numbers below describe groups, not individuals. They cannot tell you whether you have an attention problem, nor can they prove that social media causes attention problems. Each number is followed by its limits.

What We Know

Three verifiable numbers, and what each of them cannot say.

Claim 1 · Usage Behaviour
Among Hong Kong youth aged 11–24, 93.4% use social media, for an average of 4.1 hours per day.
SourceHong Kong Christian Service, "Youth Loneliness and Online Social Behaviour Survey"
Sample572 Hong Kong youth aged 11–24 (questionnaire survey)
DatePublished July 2026
DenominatorOf 572 respondents, 93.4% reported using social media; the 4.1-hour average is self-reported
What this number cannot say: It cannot describe "university students" specifically (the sample covers ages 11–24, including secondary school students and working youth); nor can it show a causal link between usage time and attention difficulties. Self-reported usage time may also be subject to recall bias.
Claim 2 · Clinical Prevalence
Among Hong Kong children and adolescents aged 6–17, the 12-month prevalence of ADHD is 10.2% (95% CI: 9.4–11.0%).
SourceHong Kong Child and Adolescent Psychiatric Epidemiology Survey (2019–2023)
SampleWeighted sample of 6,082 school children and adolescents aged 6–17
DatePublished 2025
DenominatorOf 6,082 respondents, 10.2% met DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for ADHD in the past 12 months
What this number cannot say: This is a clinical diagnosis prevalence rate, not the proportion of people who "feel their attention is poor." It also does not cover university students aged 18+. ADHD has multiple causes involving genetics, neurodevelopment, and environment; this number itself has nothing to do with social media.
Claim 3 · Trend
In the 2024/25 school year, 81.1% of Hong Kong secondary school students spent at least 2 hours on electronic screens for entertainment on a typical school day.
SourceDepartment of Health, "Student Health Service 2024/25 Annual Health Report", cited in a Legislative Council document
SampleApproximately 429,000 primary and secondary school students in Hong Kong
DateApril 2026 Legislative Council reply
DenominatorAmong surveyed secondary students, 81.1% reported spending ≥2 hours on screens for entertainment on a typical school day
What this number cannot say: "Electronic screens for entertainment" includes social media, games, videos, etc., not only social media. 2 hours is a threshold, not a continuous measure of attention. Secondary school data cannot be directly generalised to university students. A rising rate does not necessarily mean attention problems are rising.

⚠️ What Putting These Three Numbers Together Cannot Tell Us

Claim 1 is about usage behaviour, Claim 2 is about clinical diagnosis, and Claim 3 is about the trend in screen entertainment time. There is no causal evidence linking the three.

The WHO 2025 policy brief explicitly states that the relationship between technology use and mental health is bidirectional, with mixed evidence showing both negative and positive associations. A 2024 IEEE paper citing Hong Kong research found that social media and multitasking were associated with lower academic performance, but this is still correlation, not "social media causes attention deficits".

What this website can say: Long usage time and attention difficulties can coexist, but each may have separate causes. If you are concerned about your own situation, please see Find Help.
