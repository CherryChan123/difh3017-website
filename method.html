🤖 AI Use Log

The following is AI's actual involvement in this project. Each entry records: which tool was used, what the prompt was, what AI produced, what humans changed, and what was rejected.

April 2026 · Website Structure
Tool: Claude
Prompt: "Write a health communication website homepage using HTML + Bootstrap 5, topic is social media and youth attention, audience is Hong Kong university students, include a content note and skip link."
AI output: A complete `index.html` skeleton with navigation, hero section, and observation tool form.
Human edits: Removed the AI-suggested "Your attention score is XX" result display; changed it to show only an observation description, with no score.
Rejected suggestion: AI proposed adding a "Share to social media" button. Rejected — it contradicts the site's message about reducing mindless scrolling.
April 2026 · Evidence Page
Tool: Claude
Prompt: "Help me organise these three claims into a table, each with source, sample, date, and limits. Do not fill in numbers for me; the numbers I provide are all there is."
AI output: Table structure and CSS styling.
Human edits: Removed all AI-inserted "[citation:1]" markers and replaced them with readable source names. Verified every number against the original source.
Rejected suggestion: AI suggested adding the sentence "Research shows social media causes attention decline." Rejected — current evidence does not support a causal conclusion.
April 2026 · Story Page
Tool: Claude
Prompt: "Write a fictional Hong Kong university student, five scenes, showing social media use and attention difficulties. Do not diagnose, do not promise recovery, do not use the word 'addiction'."
AI output: Five-scene story draft, protagonist named Ah Ching.
Human edits: Removed the AI-written ending "she finally learned to put down her phone and her grades improved" and changed it to "she started recording her usage patterns but did not reach a conclusion." Changed scene 03 from "she felt like a failure" to "she was not angry, just tired" — avoiding self-stigmatising language.
Rejected suggestion: AI suggested adding a "Quiz: Is your attention healthy?" at the end of each scene. Rejected — the website does not diagnose.
April 2026 · Support Page
Tool: Claude
Prompt: "List verifiable mental health support hotlines and university counselling services in Hong Kong, each with operating organisation, service hours, and target group."
AI output: A list of hotlines.
Human edits: Checked every number and service hour against the organisation's official website; removed two discontinued numbers provided by AI; separated "urgent" and "non-urgent" sections.
Rejected suggestion: AI suggested adding an "online self-assessment" link. Rejected — unverified assessment tools may mislead readers.

✏️ Human Editing Principles

AI played the role of drafter, not decision-maker, in this project. The following are the final decisions retained by human editors:

  • Numbers were not outsourced: Every claim's number came from a manually verified original source, not generated by AI.
  • Causal language was not outsourced: AI repeatedly suggested "causes" and "proves"; all were changed to "associated with", "coexist", and "cannot establish causation".
  • Diagnostic language was not outsourced: The website does not contain sentences like "you have ADHD" or "you have anxiety disorder".
  • Support information was not outsourced: Hotline numbers, service hours, and target groups were all manually verified against official organisation websites.
  • Stories were not outsourced: Every scene's tone and details were rewritten by humans to ensure no promise of recovery and no stigmatisation.

⚠️ Known Limitations of This Website

  • No university-specific local data: Existing Hong Kong data cover ages 6–17 and 11–24; there is no survey specifically on "Hong Kong university students", social media, and attention. We do not pretend it exists.
  • No causal evidence: The website presents correlations and trends, not causation. The WHO 2025 policy brief explicitly notes that the relationship between technology use and mental health is bidirectional, with mixed evidence.
  • "Attention difficulties" is not a single measure: Some people mean difficulty focusing, some mean being easily distracted, some mean memory decline. The website does not combine these into a single score.
  • The story is fictional: Ah Ching is not a real person; her experiences cannot represent any reader, nor can they be used to infer population data.
  • Support information is time-sensitive: Hotline numbers and service hours may change. If a number does not work, refer to the organisation's official website.
  • This website is not a medical tool: It does not provide diagnosis, treatment advice, or clinical assessment. If you are concerned about your own situation, please seek professional support.
